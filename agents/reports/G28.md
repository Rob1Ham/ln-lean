## Reasoning

The mechanical picture is healthy: every one of the 112 required pairs exists,
every report has a `Status:` and a `Build:` line, every module has exactly one
obligation marker, and 111/112 oleans are newer than their source. The 14
sorries are exactly the sites disclosed by the reports, each adjacent to its
marker, so there is no hidden `sorry` and no false "sorry-free" claim. The one
mechanical failure is `D40`: its report's `RC 0` predates the final source
edit, so the file on disk has no successful-build evidence.

The substantive problem is the status table. Fifteen D/E rows land in
different status classes for the same catalog target, and several reports are
internally over-cautious in the body but over-optimistic on the `Status:` line
(D09, D32, E32, E45, D20). This matters because the wave plans say integration
will "compare D and E per target and keep one whole-file version"; a comparison
driven by `Status:` would select D20's construction lemma over E20's
counterexample, and E05's validity-restricted surjectivity over D05's
well-formed-counterexample. Those are the wrong choices for a faithfulness
audit: the negative result is the one that identifies the missing model field.
F is not a sorry-free hardening lane either: 10 of 16 F modules retain a
`sorry` at the known-false unconditional statement. That is allowed by the F
brief (and disclosed), but it means F cannot be advertised as closing those
obligations, only as documenting them and supplying conditional replacements.

The transient `D37Alt`/`E03Alt` files were a real integration hazard while
they existed: built, sorry-bearing, unreported, and duplicate copies of the
same targets. They were deleted during the audit, so the action is now only to
confirm they stay gone; if a root regeneration happens from a stale snapshot or
restores them, it would add a second declaration set for the same targets.
