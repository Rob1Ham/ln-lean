## Reasoning

Target 3: after the E-lane repair at 00:18–00:19, D03 and E03 are both
sorry-free with current `.olean` files and matching reports, so the earlier
build-evidence objection to E03 no longer applies. The files are now a close
tie on the brief's criteria: zero sorry sites each, equivalent statement
fidelity, the same `csv < 256` conditional, and the same `0`/`256`
counterexample. I keep D03 for three marginal, evidence-based reasons:
(i) its counterexample is a single packaged theorem with the disequality,
whereas E03 splits the equality and the `0 ≠ 256` observation;
(ii) its exact `toList` decomposition plus `determines` theorem is the cleaner
single-statement characterization of the template (E03's accessors collectively
imply the same fact); and (iii) D03 has been stable since 23:40, while E03 was
rewritten and rebuilt at 00:19 after an E03Alt candidate containing a `sorry`
was deleted. E03's 26 declarations (versus D03's 18) are not the reason: the
extra declarations are accessor lemmas, and the brief forbids choosing on
declaration count alone. E03 is a legitimate substitute if the parent prefers
an operational byte-decoder with a headline conjunction; it is not blocked.

Target 4: both D04 and E04 are build-verified and sorry-free, so this is also
a close call. I prefer D04 because it (i) cross-checks the seam constant
against both `Channel` and `Proofs.Bitcoin.WeightProps`/`weightFromBytes`,
whereas E04 stops at Seam-versus-Channel and does Appendix A as bare
arithmetic; and (ii) proves the record-level decomposition for an arbitrary
non-anchor `Commitment`, while E04's record-level results are tied to the
`zeroHtlcCommitment` constructor. E04's `base_weight_eq_iff_no_htlcs` is a
stronger exact characterization than D04's one-way bound, and its
`base_weight_fee` link is useful, so E04 is a fully acceptable fallback; those
two lemmas are worth porting if D04 is kept. The only D04 hypothesis,
`c.anchorOutputs = false`, is a faithful domain restriction, not a hidden
weakening.

Process note: E03Alt.lean was live during the audit and was inspected before
deletion. It contained a real `sorry` for a proposition it itself refuted
(`waveE_e03alt_delay_not_determined`) and a stale RC 0 report. Its deletion is
the right outcome; its orphan `.olean` should be cleaned so the build tree does
not advertise a module that no longer exists.
