# D02 — Channel safety is preserved for reorgs within the adversary's depth budget, and deep reo

## Target

- Parent/catalog: `LN-L1-003`
- Module: `LnLean.Proofs.WaveD.D02`
- File: `LnLean/Proofs/WaveD/D02.lean`

## Result

- Status: conditional (positive conditional theorem plus mechanised boundary
  counterexamples; no `sorry`)
- Build: RC 0
  (`export PATH=/dev/shm/btc/elan/toolchains/leanprover--lean4---v4.35.0/bin:$PATH && flock -w 900 /tmp/ln-build.lock lake build LnLean.Proofs.WaveD.D02`)
- Theorems/definitions:
  - `antiReorgDelay = 6` and `confirmationCount tip h = tip + 1 - h`:
    the LDK `ANTI_REORG_DELAY` constant and the model's confirmation count.
  - `reorgSurvives r tip h` (`r.depth < confirmationCount tip h`) and
    `waveD_d02_survives_iff`: a depth-`d` reorg retains the block at `h`
    exactly when `h + d ≤ tip`.
  - `OnchainEvent` (height plus value at stake), its `confirmations`,
    `survives`, and `settled` (at least `antiReorgDelay` confirmations).
  - `ChannelReorgState` (tip, `Ledger`, resolutions), `OnchainSafe`,
    `OutOfReorgReach`, `Safe`, and `applyReorg` (evicts exactly the
    resolutions that do not survive).
  - `waveD_d02_safe_preserved`: a `Safe` state stays `Safe` after any
    `Adversary.reorgAllowed` reorg.  The proof shows the resolution filter is
    the identity (`waveD_d02_applyReorg_resolutions`) and that the ledger is
    untouched (`waveD_d02_ledger_conserves_after_reorg`).
  - `waveD_d02_outOfReorgReach_of_settled` and
    `waveD_d02_safety_preserved_within_anti_reorg_delay`: if every resolution
    is settled and `a.reorgBudget < antiReorgDelay`, then every allowed reorg
    preserves safety.  This is the faithful conditional form of the catalog
    claim.
  - `waveD_d02_settled_claim_evicted_by_allowed_reorg` and
    `waveD_d02_budget_boundary`: at the exact boundary, a resolution with the
    full six confirmations does *not* survive a budget-six depth-six reorg.
    Hence the unconditional reading is false in the current model.
  - `waveD_d02_deep_reorg_evicts_settled` and
    `waveD_d02_deep_reorg_separate_failure_mode`: every reorg with
    `antiReorgDelay ≤ r.depth` evicts some resolution of a maximally settled
    channel — the separate deep-reorg failure mode.
  - `waveD_d02_no_finite_confirmation_depth`: no finite confirmation count
    defeats an unbounded reorg (for every `c`, a depth-`c` reorg evicts an
    event with exactly `c` confirmations).
  - BOLT 5 section: `waveD_d02_safeToBackFail`,
    `waveD_d02_safeToBackFail_survives`, and
    `waveD_d02_unsafeToBackFail_allowed_eviction`: back-failing an inbound
    HTLC is safe exactly when the on-chain resolution is deeper than the whole
    budget; otherwise some allowed reorg evicts it.
- Added hypotheses:
  - The preservation statement from `OnchainSafe` needs
    `a.reorgBudget < antiReorgDelay`.  This is not a missing field of
    `Adversary`: it is the library-wide anti-reorg assumption documented at
    `channelmonitor.rs:347`, and it is the exact hypothesis LDK says is needed.
  - The abstract invariant `OutOfReorgReach` is parameterised by the
    adversary's budget.  The model's `reorgAllowed` constrains depth by budget
    but places no upper bound on the budget itself.
  - The reorg step keeps the ledger unchanged; only the observable
    resolutions are filtered.  This is a modelling assumption of the new
    module, not a proved fact about `rust-lightning`.
- Statement fidelity:
  - Under the model's depth-only `Reorg`, the claim is **not** true
    unconditionally.  `reorgAllowed` is an allowance; the counterexample above
    shows an allowed reorg can evict a six-confirmation resolution.
  - The confirmation convention is explicit: the block itself counts as one
    confirmation, and a depth-`d` reorg removes blocks above `tip - d`.  Thus
    an event with `c` confirmations survives iff `d < c`.  This makes the
    boundary sharp at budget six, matching LDK's six-confirmation
    `confirmation_threshold`; LDK's prose (“deeper than six”) is informal and
    is recorded here rather than silently reconciled.
  - The theorem proves *eviction* of an on-chain resolution, not actual theft;
    loss would require L1 transaction/script semantics and the channel
    monitor's broadcast logic, neither of which is in this model.

## Remaining gap / next step

- Connect eviction to an actual loss predicate in `Bitcoin.Data.*` plus the
  `ChannelMonitor` claim/broadcast rules, so that the BOLT 5 “losing money”
  sentence and LDK's “counterparties may steal funds” become a theorem rather
  than an eviction witness.
- The wave-A `Reorg` has no replacement-chain height.  Add that field (or a
  chain-view transition) to state preservation relative to the new tip
  instead of the pre-reorg observation tip.
- Model the monitor's re-broadcast/anti-reorg bookkeeping so that the
  “separate failure mode” can be classified as recoverable (re-broadcast) or
  unrecoverable (lost penalty/claim).
