## Scope caveats

- **The length header stays plaintext and abstract.**  Noise encryption, the
  16-byte MACs, message dispatch, and message bodies are out of scope, exactly
  as in `LnLean/Wire/Message.lean` and `ConformanceDiffTargets.lean`.
- **Large cases are generic in the payload.**  No theorem forces the kernel to
  reduce a 65 KiB array; `zeros n` and hypotheses `payload.size = n` are used
  throughout.  Only the four small vectors are concrete byte arrays.
- **The production decoder is payload-only and unchanged.**  The theorem
  `unframe_frame_65535` records that `Wire.unframe` accepts a `65535`-byte
  payload even though that message does not fit the BOLT 1/8 transport limit
  (`¬ m.fits`).  This is the intended model distinction, not a claim that
  production code should accept such a message: callers must check `fits`
  separately.  `LnLean/Wire/Message.lean` was **not** modified; the promoted
  `Transport` namespace is imported as-is.
- **`production_decoder_rejects_max_transport` carries an unused size
  hypothesis** (`_h : m.payload.size = 65533`) to name the boundary case; the
  underlying fact is generic (`Transport.unframe_transportFrame_eq_none`).
- **No live/vendor claim.**  This module proves the model's accept/reject
  behaviour only; the pinned rust-lightning differential/live rows are
  unchanged and are not re-verified here.
- The concrete vectors intentionally avoid `decide` over large arrays, so they
  do not exercise the `65533`-byte body itself; that body is covered
  symbolically by `unframeTransport_transportFrame_65533` and
  `declared_65535_accepts`.
