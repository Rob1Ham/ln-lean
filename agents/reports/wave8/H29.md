## Scope caveats

1. **The law-level instantiation is definitional; the content is the
   identification.** `verifyImplLaws.sigCheck` is *defined* as
   `verifyImpl (sigBody sig) pk msg`. What is proved is that this is exactly
   the vendored seam's witness-v0 verdict at the constructed digest
   (`verifyImplLaws_sigCheck_eq_vendor_checker`), that the pre-existing
   wave-5/6 `vendorLaws` is the same function while ignoring its `msg`
   (`vendorLaws_sigCheck_eq_verifyImpl`), and that model acceptance composes to
   vendored `.ok`.
2. **No positive EC witness is mechanized.** `verifyImpl` runs 256-bit
   secp256k1 arithmetic (mod-n inverse, scalar multiplication). This module
   proves no concrete `verifyImpl sig pk msg = true`; the positive direction
   remains conditional on the verifier returning `true` (the exact algebraic
   face of assumption C3), now stated at the vendor function. Only the
   negative side is concrete (`verifyImpl_sig_nil`, `verifyImpl_pk_nil`,
   one-byte signatures and empty pubkeys).
3. **No negative direction.** The vendored agreement theorems are
   one-directional (`model ⇒ vendor .ok`). No theorem in this tree gives
   `vendor .ok ⇒ model Accepts`, so no `verifyWitnessProgram ... ≠ .ok`
   statement is claimed. `vendor_checker_empty_sig_false` is a
   *checker*-level negative only.
4. **Message/sighash premises.** `D` must equal
   `sighashWitnessV0 tx nIn scriptCode (hashtypeOf sig) amount` for each
   signature (the funding theorem takes `hD1`/`hD2` separately; the two hash
   types may differ). The trailing signature byte is the hash type
   (`hashtypeOf`), and the digest is 32 bytes
   (`verifyImplLaws_digest_size`). The model `msg` is consumed by the law;
   `vendorLaws` did not consume it, which is why waves 6–7 had to construct the
   digest inside the checker.
5. **Key-encoding premises.** The seam's `CPubKey::IsValid` gate is explicit
   (`Bitcoin.Crypto.Seam.Der.isValidPubKey pk = true`), together with the
   wave-5 33-byte key sizes; without the gate the model law could accept an
   encoding the vendor seam rejects.
6. **Lax DER, not BIP 66.** `verifyImpl` is the lax `parseDerLax` EC layer;
   strict-DER/policy rejection lives in the interpreter flags above it. The
   `isLowDERSignature`/NULLDUMMY policy layer is unchanged and not claimed.
7. **Context is still explicit.** Spent `amount`, `scriptCode`, `nIn`,
   sequence environment and transaction validity are inputs; the digest
   commits to them but nothing here proves they match the referenced outpoint.
   `to_local` retains the wave-7 `csv < 32768`/decode/disable-bit premises
   (`hdec`, `hnn`, `hdis`).
8. Inherited `SpendTargets` simplifications: model truthiness (non-minimal
   `false` is truthy) and 2-of-2-only `OP_CHECKMULTISIG`. `verifyImplLaws` does
   not change them.

## Blockers / missing link

- **Positive EC instance.** There is no kernel-cheap witness that
  `verifyImpl sig pk msg = true`; a concrete vector would require evaluating
  256-bit secp256k1 arithmetic in the kernel (or trusting a vendor test
  theorem). This is the single missing link between “model acceptance ⇒
  vendor `.ok`” and an unconditional positive spend example.
- **Negative direction.** The wave-5/6 agreement theorems are not
  biconditionals; proving `vendor .ok ⇒ model Accepts` would require the
  converse of the whole interpreter computation, which is not available.
- **Checker transfer.** `txCheckerFor`/`realCheckerOf` instantiation is owned by
  the wave-8 `TxValidation.Full` lanes (`H01`, `H30`–`H32`), not this lane.
