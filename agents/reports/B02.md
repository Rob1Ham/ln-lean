## 4. What remains open

1. **LN-SCRIPT-001** (`pushScriptNum32_injective`). The fixed-width
   `pushUInt32LE` encoding is injective on `< 2^32`, but the proof needs a
   byte-extensionality argument over `pushData`. It is the smallest next
   target.
2. **LN-SCRIPT-002** and **LN-SCRIPT-003** lift that injectivity to the
   received-HTLC CLTV branch and the offered-HTLC payment-hash branch. They
   require showing that the relevant encoding occupies a fixed offset inside
   the otherwise identical scripts (`ByteArray.extract`/`getElem` lemmas).
3. **Hash bridge.** `CommitmentParams.HashesMatch` is not proved: the model
   takes witness-script hashes and 20-byte `HASH160` digests as parameters.
   Connecting them to `SHA256`/`RIPEMD160` is assumption C1/C2 and belongs to
   the crypto workstream.
4. **Script semantics.** There is no interpreter, signature check, or
   spendability proof. Consequently the revocation path, CSV enforcement,
   CLTV enforcement, and the anchor's anyone-can-spend-after-16-blocks path
   are only template-level here.
5. **Encoding exactness.** `pushScriptNum16`/`pushScriptNum32` are
   fixed-width. The proved sizes 77/136/143 are exact for this scaffold
   encoding, not for BOLT 3's minimal `CScriptNum` encoding for all values.
6. **Output construction beyond the zero-HTLC anchors case.**
   `commitmentOutputs` emits exactly four outputs in template order. HTLC
   output values, BIP 69+CLTV ordering, dust trimming, fee deduction, the
   `shared_anchor` / `zero_fee_commitments` variant, and the BOLT 3 rule
   that omits one anchor when the corresponding commitment output is absent
   are outside this file. In particular the model always emits both anchors.

Next smallest step: prove `pushScriptNum32_injective` by `ByteArray.ext`
(`pushUInt32LE a` has exactly four bytes, and `getElem` at offsets 0..3 is
the corresponding byte of `a`), then use fixed-offset extraction to discharge
LN-SCRIPT-002/003.
