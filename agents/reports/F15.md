# F15 — Keysend preimage binding

## Target
- Marker: `LN-WF-F15`
- Module: `LnLean.Proofs.ModelHardening.F15`
- Parent obligation: `LN-PM-KS` (proof-side companion of `LN-PM-006`)
- Imports: `LnLean.Payment.HTLC`, `LnLean.Payment.Invoice`,
  `LnLean.Proofs.Payment.KeysendProps`. The brief's `LnLean.Payment.HTL` is a
  typo; the model file is `LnLean/Payment/HTLC.lean`.

## Result
- Status: conditional (main theorem) + counterexample (unconditional form)
- Build: RC 0
- New definitions:
  - `IsCommittedPreimage sha256 h p := sha256 p = h` — the hash/preimage
    commitment, independent of session or settlement state.
  - `SenderPreimageBinding sha256 k := IsCommittedPreimage sha256
    k.session.paymentHash k.preimage` — the named sender-preimage binding
    predicate for a keysend record.
  - `AcceptedKeysend sha256 k := k.Valid sha256 ∧ k.session.state = .pending`
    — the missing acceptance premise: the recipient's check passed and the
    payment has not settled.
- Main theorems:
  - `binding_iff_preimageCommits` — the new predicate is exactly
    `PaymentSession.preimageCommits` with the record's preimage.
  - `valid_iff_binding_and_amountBound` — `Keysend.Valid` is precisely the
    binding plus the BOLT 2 amount bound (the new predicate isolates the hash
    half without weakening acceptance).
  - `acceptedKeysend_iff` — acceptance = binding + amount bound + pending.
  - `bound_settle_reveals_committed` — **strongest main result**: the
    binding predicate plus pending state alone suffice for successful
    settlement to reveal `some k.preimage`, committed to by the
    acceptance-time payment hash, and still committed after settlement. The
    amount bound is not needed.
  - `accepted_settle_reveals_committed` — the accepted-keysend form of the
    same result, derived from the full `Keysend.Valid` certificate.
  - `accepted_any_settlement_reveal_binds` — any settlement outcome that
    reveals a preimage reveals the accepted one, committed to the
    acceptance-time hash.
  - `accepted_trace_reveal_binds` — the same for every preimage revealed by
    an arbitrary settlement trace (`applyOutcomes`); no C1 assumption needed.
  - `accepted_same_hash_same_reveal` — under `CollisionFree sha256`, two
    accepted keysends sharing a payment hash cannot reveal different
    preimages (uses `keysend_preimage_unique`).
  - `binding_preserved_by_settle`, `binding_preserved_by_retry` — the
    invariant survives settlement and LDK-style retry.
  - `unchecked_record_counterexample` — mechanized witness that an unchecked
    pending record can settle successfully and reveal a preimage different
    from the genuinely committed one.
  - `uncheckedKeysend_not_valid` — the same record fails `Keysend.Valid`, so
    the acceptance hypothesis is not redundant.
- Added hypotheses:
  - `AcceptedKeysend` (equivalently `k.Valid sha256` plus pending) for the
    main theorem; this is the recipient check modelled by wave A.
  - `CollisionFree sha256` only for the uniqueness/equivocation corollary.
  - No hypothesis on `sha256` for the positive binding direction.
- Statement fidelity:
  - The conditional statement is the faithful protocol claim: after
    acceptance, settlement reveals the preimage committed by the accepted
    `update_add_htlc` hash.
  - The unconditional statement over raw `Keysend` records is false and is
    retained verbatim as `unconditional_settle_reveals_committed` with one
    `sorry` and the marker directly beside it (theorem at line 338, marker
    and `sorry` at lines 344–348). It is not silently weakened.
  - `unchecked_record_counterexample` proves the existential failure of that
    unconditional form with concrete values, so the negative result is
    mechanized rather than asserted.
  - `exampleHash` (bytes embedded in the payment hash) is an illustrative
    stand-in only; no SHA-256 or computational claim is made.

## Missing model field / next step

`Keysend.settle` and `Keysend.revealedPreimage` never consult `Keysend.Valid`.
The raw `PaymentSession` records the payment hash, target, parts, and state,
but not that acceptance checked `sha256(preimage) = payment_hash`; the
preimage lives only in the outbound `Keysend` record. Consequently settlement
will expose any stored preimage on `succeeded`, checked or not.

A model-hardening change would be to carry an acceptance certificate in the
session/record (e.g. a `CheckedPreimage` field produced only by the LDK check)
and make `settle` consume it. That is a model edit and was out of scope here.
Until then, use `AcceptedKeysend`/`SenderPreimageBinding` as the reusable
premise for keysend payment proofs. The parent `LN-PM-006` remains
statement-only in the global obligation catalog because its model declaration
`LnLean.Payment.Keysend.preimage_binding` does not exist; this module supplies
the proved conditional content under new names.
