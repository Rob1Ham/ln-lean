# G05 — Audit targets D09/E09 and D10/E10

## Verdict
- Recommendation: **Target 9 — keep E09** (`ln-lean-wavee/LnLean/Proofs/WaveE/E09.lean`) as the best available whole file; D09 is a close second.  Target 9 is **not** a discharge of LN-CR-005 at the BOLT parser level and should remain partial/blocked until the model parser checks ordering.  **Target 10 — keep D10** (`ln-lean/LnLean/Proofs/WaveD/D10.lean`); it has no correctness blocker, only a traceability/model-name gap.
- Confidence: **medium** (high on the sorry/build facts; medium on the close D09/E09 choice).
- Blocking issues:
  1. **LN-CR-005 is definitional, not substantive, in both files.**  `LnLean/Wire/TLV.lean:96` defines `TLVStream.WellFormed s := s.Sorted ∧ s.Unique`, exactly the conclusion of the catalog claim.  Consequently `waveD_d09_wellFormed_iff` and `waveE_e09_wellFormed_iff` are `Iff.rfl`; every positive theorem assumes `h : s.WellFormed`.  That is faithful to the catalog's `lean_target`, but it proves the predicate, not the BOLT 1 parser guarantee.
  2. **The model parser violates the BOLT 1 receiver rule.**  `refs/bolts/01-messaging.md:164-166` says non-strictly-increasing decoded TLV types MUST fail to parse.  `LnLean/Wire/TLV.lean:181-208` (`parseRecord`, `decodeTLVGo`) contains no such check, even though the module docstring at `TLV.lean:4-8` says the decoder rejects repeated/non-increasing types.  Both D09 and E09 mechanize the contradiction with the same `0x01 0x01 0x2a 0x01 0x01 0x2b` byte stream.  Until `decodeTLV` validates ordering, no theorem `decodeTLV b = some s → s.WellFormed` can exist.
  3. **LN-CR-006 has no model declaration named `decode_length`.**  `docs/TRACEABILITY.md:25` and `agents/status/obligations.json` still point at `LnLean.Wire.Message.decode_length`, which is absent from the model.  D10 supplies the byte-array projection `declaredLength`; E10 supplies the list projection `decodeLength`.  The traceability entry must be remapped or a model declaration added.
  4. **No F equivalent exists for either target.**  There is no `D09Alt.lean`, `E09Alt.lean`, `D10Alt.lean`, or `E10Alt.lean` in any of the three worktrees.  Wave F has `ModelHardening/F09.lean` and `F10.lean`, but they are unrelated obligations (`LN-CH-DUST` and `LN-CH-005`) and each contains a `sorry` at `F09.lean:472` and `F10.lean:473`.

## Reasoning

Target 9 is a tie on the mechanical criteria: both files have zero proof-site `sorry`, both have a matching `.olean`/`.trace` after their source, both state the same `WellFormed` predicate, and neither adds a hypothesis to the main claim.  The choice therefore turns on content and counterexample quality.  E09 is the better keep: its counterexample is existential over the input byte array (`waveE_e09_decoded_not_wellFormed`) and its `waveE_e09_not_all_wellFormed` states the direct negation of the universal reading; D09 fixes the counterexample byte array in the statement.  E09 also adds a genuine closure theorem (`append_wellFormed`) and record-level uniqueness (`record_eq_of_type_eq`).  D09's main content advantages are index-level order/injectivity (`getElem_lt`, `type_getElem_injective`) plus a single stream-level `WellFormed ↔ types.Pairwise` theorem (`waveD_d09_wellFormed_iff_pairwise`); E09's list-level pairwise directions and `type_count_le_one` cover the same no-duplicate/order content, though not the single combined stream-level statement.  E09 is not chosen for declaration count; D09 actually has fewer declarations, and the two files prove the same core facts.

Target 10 also ties on sorry/build/fidelity.  D10 is the better keep because it proves the catalog claim at the same byte-array API as `unframe` (`waveD_d10_decode_declared_length`), gives the strongest single characterization of the decoder (`waveD_d10_decode_some_iff`), and is the more explicit about the model/BOLT transport-length mismatch.  E10 has the better explicit rejection family (`rejects_mismatch`, `trailing_bytes_rejected`, `short_frame_rejected`), but those are consequences of D10's exact acceptance characterization; D10's `decode_wrong_length_none` is narrower but the exact `decode_some_iff` covers the general case.  E10 is a valid alternate if the integrator prefers explicit negative lemmas over the stronger positive characterization.

Neither target-9 file can honestly be recorded as a BOLT-level proof of LN-CR-005.  Both prove the model predicate and then prove that the model's parser does not enforce it.  That is useful, honest work, but the catalog claim as a receiving-node requirement remains false in the model.  Both agent reports say "Status: proved"; that is accurate only at the predicate level, not for the decoder requirement.  Target 10 is model-level true because `unframeList` rejects on the length mismatch; its remaining issue is traceability, not correctness.
