## Verdict
- Recommendation: **Target 41 — keep `D` (`LnLean/Proofs/WaveD/D41.lean`); target 42 — keep `D` (`LnLean/Proofs/WaveD/D42.lean`).** Do not select `E41`/`E42` as the primary whole-file versions. No `F41`/`F42` replacement exists; `F01` is a complementary hardening module for target 41 only.

## Reasoning
- **Do not rank on declaration count.** D41 has 43 source-level `theorem`/`def` declarations versus E41's 24, and D42 has 40 versus E42's 19, but the decision below rests on theorem content, hypotheses, and counterexamples.
- **Target 41: D41.** Because both files' `PrimitiveModel`-based SHA-256 theorems are vacuous, the choice must rest on the unconditional and negative content, where D41 is stronger. Its tie-breakers are the mechanized negative results: `waveD_d41_witnessScriptHash_underdetermined` constructs two well-formed `FundingParams` with the same 2-of-2 witness script and different 32-byte `witnessScriptHash` values, and `waveD_d41_no_global_sha256_bridge` derives from it that no single hash function can satisfy the bridge for every well-formed record. That is exactly the model gap the catalog claim hides, and it is proved rather than asserted. D41 also has the parser-level decomposition and the unique-program injectivity, so it covers the "decomposition" wording of target 41 more completely. E41 is cleaner in its hypothesis set and its `waveE_e41_decomposition_2of2` is the most literal rendering of the BOLT sentence, but its only evidence about the missing bridge is prose, and its SHA-256 theorems inherit the same `PrimitiveModel` vacuity. D41's own `waveD_d41_revealed_script_eq_witnessScript` is an extra theorem that can be deleted or replaced by F01's restricted statement; it does not affect any decomposition theorem. I therefore keep D41 and treat F01 as a complementary hardening module, not a replacement.
- **Target 42: D42.** Fidelity to the BOLT `to_local` template is stronger in D42: it proves the exact five-byte delay chunk at fixed offsets 36–40 including `OP_CSV` at 39 and `OP_DROP` at 40, and it provides the explicit link from `Commitment.contestDelay` to the bytes at 37/38. It also proves the modulo-`2^16` statement for arbitrary delays, which makes the limitation precise. E42's advantages are real but lower-ranked: fewer hypotheses (no 33-byte key requirement for its core lemmas), a key-parametric collision counterexample, and cleaner documentation. Since the ranking weights statement fidelity above counterexample quality, D42 is the better whole file. The choice is medium-confidence because D42's header contains the false "`d ≥ 128` is byte-identical to minimal encoding" sentence; the theorem statements remain correct, and the sentence is a one-line documentation fix. If the integrator will not permit that fix, E42 is the defensible fallback.
- **No F/replacement/blocked verdict for whole files.** There is no `F41.lean`/`F42.lean`; `F01` is a different obligation (`LN-WF-F01`) and proves only the funding-hash bridge slice. No file is missing, so "blocked" is not warranted for selecting a whole file. The SHA-256 identification itself is effectively blocked until the `PrimitiveModel` interface is repaired or the claim is explicitly restated as an idealised bare-hash statement; that is a model-level issue shared by D41/E41 and outside this audit's write scope. E41/E42 are sound, sorry-free, and integrate against byte-identical shared modules; they are simply second-best for these two targets.
