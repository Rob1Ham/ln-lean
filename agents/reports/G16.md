## Reasoning

For target 31 the decision is not about proved content: both files are sorry-free, build, and their central theorem is the same one-line projection of `Keysend.Valid.hashIsPreimage` that `KeysendProps` already exposes as `Keysend.preimageCommits`. The deciding difference is catalog fidelity. The obligation's `leanTarget` is the literal name `LnLean.Payment.Keysend.preimage_binding`; E31 declares exactly that name at `E31.lean:198` and also states the trace form and the honest split between the unconditional operational claim and the conditional uniqueness claim. D31 is a clean, self-contained family and its `settle_preserves_binding` and unchecked-record witness are useful, but it deliberately leaves the catalog target undefined, so keeping D31 leaves the obligation "statement-only" unless someone adds a wrapper. Keep E31 as the whole file; treat D31 as a redundant second family. Since E31 already imports `KeysendProps`, `Keysend.preimageCommits` remains available for callers and D31's best lemma (`settle_preserves_binding`) can be regenerated in two lines if needed.

The F15 companion cannot be the kept whole file: it has one intentional `sorry` (`F15.lean:348`, and `sorryAx` in its `.olean`), so it fails the "actual sorry sites" criterion outright and should stay out of the no-sorry gate. What it does better than both lanes is the negative result: `unchecked_record_counterexample` shows the operational consequence of the missing acceptance field (an unchecked keysend settles and reveals an uncommitted preimage), and `bound_settle_reveals_committed` isolates the hash binding from the amount bound. Those declarations should be ported into the kept module or into a sorry-free model-hardening follow-up; they should not be lost with the lane.

For target 32 both files are sound, but they answer different halves of the sentence. D32 is the only one whose headline puts the channel aggregate and an L1 output+fee aggregate in one equation, which is what `LN-SEC-002` says and what the BOLT 3 anchor (`chan_utils.rs:716`, commitment output values) is about; it also reuses the committed `CommitmentConserved`/`ChannelLedger` vocabulary of the catalog's target module. Its cost is a strong, order-sensitive bridge and a near-tautological canonical construction. E32 has the better L1 *model* (one valuation function, `Perm` decomposition, `others`, funding-outpoint distinctness) and the stronger dynamic result (conservation and no-double-spend preserved by every step and along reachable runs), but its `Conserves` predicate has no L1 field and its L1 theorem compares channel balances to L1 input valuations, so as written it does not state "total value is conserved across ... the L1 spend set" — it cannot, because it has no L1 output values. Fidelity therefore favours D32; generality and modelling cleanliness favour E32. Fidelity is the criterion this audit weights, so keep D32, and port E32's `SpendSetMatches`/`OutRefValue`/reachability material rather than keeping a second `ChannelAccount`/`Network` model in the tree.

Do not keep both members of either pair: the two lanes define competing `ChannelAccount` structures and competing L1 value models (per-spend `inputValueSat` vs total `OutRef → Nat`), and the traceability index would have to choose one target declaration anyway. There is no F-lane alternative for 32 to consider.
